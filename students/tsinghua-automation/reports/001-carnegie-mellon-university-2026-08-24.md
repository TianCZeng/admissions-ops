## A. Program and intake summary

Carnegie Mellon University's Master of Science in Computer Vision is a 16-month, full-time professional master's degree in the Robotics Institute, entered here for the Fall 2027 intake with no material applicant category. The official program page states that the courses are the focus of the program, complemented by a culminating experience of a hands-on team project, and the curriculum page puts the degree at 111 units across three semesters and one summer, including three units of computer-vision-relevant summer internship required for graduation. The core is built to address the two main areas the program identifies in current computer vision systems, recognition and geometry, the latter explicitly including multi-view reconstruction and SLAM. One core option pairs 16-822 Geometry-based Methods in Vision with 16-833 Robot Localization and Mapping.

Two characteristics of the program matter more for this applicant than the schedule does, and both come from the official FAQ. MSCV is not research-based, offers no master's thesis, and states that it should not be viewed as a preparatory degree program for earning a PhD. It also offers no financial aid or scholarship funding, and expects accepted students to be fully self-supported. Both facts are load-bearing in sections D, E, and F below.
