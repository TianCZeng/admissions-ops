# Statement Of Purpose Draft: Carnegie Mellon University - Master of Science in Computer Vision

**Review status:** Review Required

As an undergraduate research assistant from 2025-2027, I work on Open-Vocabulary Object Detection for Indoor Service Robots. That project and my summer research intern work in 2026 on Domain Shift Analysis for Night-Time Driving Scene Segmentation point to the same problem: vision systems break when the scene changes. In the driving project, I focused on how segmentation degrades around small objects, glare, and low-light boundaries; in the robot project, I am studying how open-vocabulary detectors transfer from web-scale image data to robot-collected indoor scenes with clutter and occlusion. Those experiences are why I want graduate study now: I want deeper technical training in domain adaptation, open-vocabulary models, and evaluation methods before I decide between a PhD path and research-focused industry work.

My preparation at the National University of Singapore has been deliberately technical. I have taken Machine Learning, Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition, Deep Learning for Visual Recognition, and Robotics Perception, and I have used that coursework to build projects that emphasize careful measurement: the Robot Vision Benchmark Toolkit for object detection on indoor robot camera frames, and the Visual Model Error Review Dashboard for annotating failures and comparing reviewer rubrics. I am more interested in understanding which errors matter and why than in reporting accuracy alone.

My capstone, Efficient Visual Retrieval for Museum Collections, extends that same habit of evaluation. I am building a retrieval prototype that tracks precision, recall, and curator-reviewed failure modes so that the system stays tied to real user feedback. That focus on measurement and interpretability is also visible in my research practice. I want a master's environment that keeps pushing me toward rigorous experiments, careful error analysis, and better questions about how models behave under uncertainty.

Carnegie Mellon is attractive because the Robotics Institute keeps computer vision connected to real perception problems. MSCV matches the questions I want to keep asking: open-vocabulary models, domain shift, 3D scene understanding, and vision-language models. In practice, I want to build perception systems that can handle novel objects, changing environments, and uncertain evidence without collapsing into brittle predictions. The program is a strong next step because it can deepen the technical base behind those questions while keeping the work grounded in deployment-relevant computer vision.

If I am admitted, I would bring a record of project work that links coursework, benchmarking, and research to one consistent goal: making visual systems more robust under distribution shift. That is the direction I want to carry into graduate study, whether I continue toward a PhD or move into research work in perception.
